## Ferrous metals

Low carbon steel
- Low carbon steel is malleable, ductile and relatively inexpensive compared to other metals
- Applications include nuts, bolts, and automotive parts
medium carbon steel
- It is hard, malleable, tough with good tensile strength
- Applications include springs, gears, rail tracks and machine parts

Grey cast iron
- It is very hard and has good resistance to oxidation
- However, it is brittle and has low impact resistance
White cast iron
- Like grey cast iron, it is also hard and brittle but has a greater tensile strength
- It can be cast with little shrinkage


## Non Ferrous Metals
Copper
- is malleable and extremely ductile
- Excellent conductor of heat and electricity
- pipes & wires
Zinc
- Is versatile but is often alloyed
- it is ductile
- A layer of oxide gives it excellent corrosion resistance - galvanization
Silver
- it is the best electric & thermal conductor (out of the metals we look at)
- it is extremely ductile
- electronics mirrors & jewelry
Gold
- gold is one of the least reactive metals
- it odes not tarnish
- verry malleable
titanium 
- low density durable metal with a high melting point
- resistance to corrosion
- is body safe and used in replacements
Tin 
- is soft and malleable
- it has good corrosion resistance
- tin plate coating & solder


## Ferrous Alloys
Stainless steel
- Is a mixture of iron carbon & chromium
- Resistance to rust and corrosion
Die Steel 
-  iron, carbon, chromium, and vanadium 
- Is used in dies (wow)
- Has high toughness, wear & heat resistance
High Speed Steel
- Has a higher hardness when at high temperature compared to other resources
-  iron, tungsten, chromium, (molybdenum, vanadium, or cobalt), carbon 

## Non Ferrous Alloys
Bronze
- Copper & Tin
- Uses in old mirrors coins, various small parts, musical instruments
- Similar properties to Tin & copper
Brass (create reference)
- Copper & zinc
- similar properties & usage to Bronze
- is more corrosion resistant so is more commonly used
Duralumin
- Duralumin (also called duraluminum, duraluminium, duralum, dural(l)ium, or dural) 
- Copper & aluminium
- Used in airframes however is susceptible to corrosion
Pewter
- Tin Antimony copper
- low melting point can be easily cast
### Stock Forms
Sheet
- Available in large panels up to 3mm thick
- Pressing punching bending spinning & riveting
Plates
- Thickness is greater than 3mm, smaller than sheet
- laser cutting, plasma cutting, frame cutting
Bar
- Flat round square & hexagonal bars
- milled, drilled, welded or turned
Tube
- Available in all stock forms as the bar
- Rectangular tube is called a box section
- Tubular stock forms are usually strong
Profile stock forms
 - angle, Tee, Chanel, I beam, H beam, wide flange
