## Vacuum Forming Process
- Once the mould has been produced there are three main stages
- A sheet of thermoplastic is heated and pressed into the former by atmospheric pressure as a vacuum reduces the pressure below the mould
#### Mould Making
- To ensure a good product is formed the mould must
	- Have corners and edges rounded with a small radius
	- have a positive draft angle
	- etc.
## Line Bending Process
- Marking out should be done careful before it is placed on the heater

## Laminating or Layup
- Thermosetting polymer resin can be combined with fibres to produce a variety of products like boats
- Fibre strands or matting like fibreglass or carbon fibre
- Matting is laid into a mould and resin is mixed with a catalyst and brushed onto the matting

## ThermoForming
- Very similar to vacuum forming however uses a male and female mould
- Forming a heated, softened polymer sheet using a vacuum

## Injection Moulding
- Two part mould are made in steel
- many polymers are suitable
-  high setup cost

## Blow moulding
- Blow moulding uses a two part mould and compressed air to make hollow shapes like bottles bins etc.

## Rotational Moulding
- Like blow moulding, rotational moulding is used to create hollow plastic forms
- The process produces a seamless product
- The plastic is placed inside a mould and heated. The mould is then rotated so that the plastic is pushed to the edges of the mould

Compression moulding 
- Is used to from thermosetting polymers using a hydraulic press
- Used to form thinks like the bottom of rubber souls
- A female and male mould are heated and pressed together
