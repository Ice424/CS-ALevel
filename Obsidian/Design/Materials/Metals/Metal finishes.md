## Cellulose and Acrylic Paint
- Paints provide both colour and a barrier against corrosion
- it needs to be prepared
- Cellulose paint dries faster due to solvents 
- Acrylic paint are water-based
	- They usually require heat professional equipment 
	- they require 24 hours to dry
	- they can be used DIY
	- can be applied in various ways


## Electroplating
- it uses a desired metal to coat a cheaper one
- can be for aesthetics or for a protective layer
- uses electrolysis
- The anodes (positive) will be the same metal as the electrolyte which you want to plate the cathode (negative)
- some metals will not alloy
## Galvanizing
- Galvanizing coats iron or steel with zinc to form a protective layer
- it can be applied using electroplating
- Larger items are usually galvanized via hot dipping
- Large pieces are dipped into a molten bath at 830 degrees
- The pieces are cleaned and checked for an even coating

## Polymer Coating
- Dip coating provides a thick ware resistant, water resistant and air tight Finnish
- The metal is heated to 230 degrees it is then dipped into a tank of fluidized polymer powder as warm air blows around it

## Sealants and Preservatives
- Sealants are used to fill air tight gaps between metals or to bond to different materials
- Preservatives prevent the meal from oxidising by having a physical barrier these are waxes, paints, and oils
## Anodizing
- Anodizing is an electrolysis process that involves thickening the oxide layer found on most metals however anodizing is most typically used on aluminium

## Plating
- can also refer to galvanising or electroplating
- Plating involves using a standard chemical reaction to adhere a different type of metal to the surface
- The reaction uses a reduction agent that reacts with both metals in an aqueous solution to release hydrogen and the desired coated metal 

## Powder Coating
- Powder coating provides a usually plastic layer to any metal surface
- The fine particles of plastic are electrically charged and sprayed onto the metal which is grounded
- You can use thermoforming, thermosetting or UV curable plastic 
- The plastic layer is usually 0.05 and 0.08mm thick
- The plastic can be glossy matt or any colour depending on the desired aesthetics

## Cathodic Protection
- Used to prevent corrosion it requires a "sacrificial" piece of metal to act as an anode in an for of ongoing electrolysis
- The metal that you want to protect acts as a cathode preventing oxidisation and encouraging reduction
